SECTION B: PROBLEM & CHALLENGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

9. What specific agri-food system problem are you addressing? Clearly describe
   the problem, its causes, and why existing approaches are insufficient. *

   White rust, caused by Albugo candida, is the most damaging disease of Indian
   mustard (Brassica juncea), causing 20-60% yield loss under favourable
   conditions. India cultivates over 6 million hectares of mustard, contributing
   27% of national oilseed production, and the National Mission on Edible Oils
   (NMEO-OS) has prioritised mustard for reducing edible-oil import dependency.
   White rust is therefore a direct threat to national food and oilseed security.

   The disease presents a unique detection challenge: pustules form on the lower
   (abaxial) leaf surface, making them invisible to overhead UAV sensors. By the
   time secondary symptoms (chlorosis, staghead) become visible from above, yield
   loss is already locked in. The biotrophic oomycete produces oospores that
   survive 20+ years in soil, and favourable conditions (12-24 deg C, morning RH
   >97%) trigger rapid epidemics with a latent period of just 9-12 days.

   Current approaches are insufficient for three reasons:
   (1) Manual scouting is slow, subjective, and covers <5% of fields. By the
       time scouts identify infected plots, the window for effective fungicide
       application has often passed.
   (2) Existing UAV-based disease detection studies overwhelmingly focus on
       single-sensor approaches (RGB or multispectral alone), missing the
       complementary information that thermal, hyperspectral, and texture
       features provide about different dimensions of the disease response.
   (3) No multimodal sensor fusion framework exists for white rust in mustard.
       While fusion has shown 10-15% accuracy improvement for wheat diseases
       (Feng et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2024), no study has applied this to the
       mustard-white rust pathosystem, which has fundamentally different
       symptomatology (abaxial pustules, staghead formation).

   The result: mustard farmers either spray entire fields uniformly (wasting
   fungicide and money) or spray too late (losing yield). There is no precision
   tool that tells them where disease is, how severe it is, and where to spray.

SECTION D: PROPOSED SOLUTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

15. Describe your proposed solution, including what you propose to develop, how
    it works, the role of AI, intended users, and expected outcomes. *

    RustWatch is a drone-based AI pipeline that fuses data from four UAV sensors
    to detect white rust and map its severity across mustard fields, producing
    actionable spray prescriptions for farmers.

    What we propose to develop:
    An end-to-end system that takes multi-sensor UAV imagery as input and
    produces a geo-referenced disease severity map with zone-wise spray
    recommendations (spray / monitor / skip) as output.

    How it works (4 stages):
    Stage 1 — Multi-sensor data acquisition: Four UAV-mounted sensors capture
    complementary disease signals: RGB (canopy texture and visual symptoms),
    multispectral (chlorophyll and pigment changes), hyperspectral (subtle
    biochemical shifts), and thermal (stomatal closure and transpiration stress).

    Stage 2 — Disease-specific feature extraction and selection: From each
    sensor, we extract candidate features (GLCM texture from RGB, vegetation
    indices from multispectral/hyperspectral, temperature parameters from
    thermal). A rigorous selection pipeline (ANOVA screening, spectral
    separability analysis, VIF for multicollinearity, and Sequential Floating
    Forward Selection) identifies the minimal set of Disease-Specific Features
    (DSFs) that maximally separate severity classes.

    Stage 3 — AI-based feature-level fusion and classification: DSFs from all
    four sensors are concatenated into a unified feature matrix, normalised,
    and passed through a second round of SFFS to find the optimal cross-modal
    feature combination. Machine learning classifiers (Random Forest, SVM, KNN)
    then classify each plot into one of six severity classes (Healthy to Very
    High) based on this fused feature set.

    Stage 4 — Severity map and spray prescription: Classification results are
    mapped back to plot geo-coordinates, producing a colour-coded severity map.
    Each severity zone is translated into a spray recommendation: Healthy/Low =
    no action, Moderate = monitor, High/Very High = spray immediately.

    Role of AI:
    AI drives the entire analytical pipeline — from automated feature selection
    (SFFS uses classifier accuracy as its objective function) to multi-class
    severity classification. The feature-level fusion is AI-guided: the algorithm
    discovers which cross-sensor feature combinations provide the greatest
    discriminative power, a task impossible through manual analysis of 50+
    candidate features.

    Intended users:
    - Drone service providers and FPOs who fly UAVs over farmer fields and need
      automated disease diagnosis
    - Agricultural extension officers (KVKs) who advise farmers on spray timing
    - Researchers studying crop disease epidemiology
    - State agriculture departments for district-level crop health monitoring

    Expected outcomes:
    - A validated multimodal fusion pipeline achieving higher classification
      accuracy than any single-sensor approach (target: 10-15% improvement,
      consistent with Feng et al., 2022)
    - A geo-referenced severity map with 6-class resolution
    - Spray prescriptions that reduce fungicide use by 15-30% through targeted
      application
    - A benchmark dataset and methodology transferable to other crop-disease
      systems

17. Briefly describe your proposed technical approach. *

    Our technical approach has two objectives executed sequentially:

    Objective 1 — Characterise white rust through disease-specific features:
    We fly four UAV sensors (RGB: DJI Phantom 4 Pro; Multispectral: MicaSense
    RedEdge MX, 5 bands; Hyperspectral: Headwall Co-Aligned VNIR-SWIR, 491
    bands; Thermal: FLIR Vue Pro R) over a controlled field experiment at IARI
    (3 varieties x 4 inoculation timings x 3 replications = 48 plots). From
    each sensor we extract candidate features:
    - RGB: 8 GLCM texture features (mean, variance, homogeneity, contrast,
      dissimilarity, entropy, second moment, correlation)
    - Multispectral: 12 vegetation indices (NDVI, GNDVI, NDRE, SAVI, OSAVI,
      CIrededge, CIgreen, TCARI, MCARI, MTCI, PSRI, MSR)
    - Hyperspectral: 30+ VIs (PRI, WI, ARI, SIPI, NPQI, RARSa/b/c, etc.),
      spectral derivatives, and continuum removal features
    - Thermal: CTD, CTR, NRCT, and thermal GLCM features

    These 50+ candidate features are then screened through a 4-step selection
    pipeline (ANOVA at p<0.05, Bhattacharyya separability analysis, VIF<10 for
    multicollinearity, SFFS with classifier objectives) to identify
    Disease-Specific Features (DSFs) per sensor — following the DSSF framework
    of Tian et al. (2021).

    Objective 2 — Detect and discriminate severity through feature-level fusion:
    DSFs from all four sensors are concatenated into a unified feature matrix
    (plot-level tabular data, n=48). After normalisation and a second VIF pass,
    SFFS selects the optimal cross-modal feature combination using RF/SVM/KNN
    classifier accuracy as the objective. We systematically compare:
    - 4 single-sensor baselines (RGB only, MS only, HS only, Thermal only)
    - 4 fusion combinations (RGB+Thermal, MS+Thermal, HS+Thermal, all four)
    Each combination is evaluated with 5-fold stratified cross-validation using
    OA, F1-score, ROC-AUC for classification (6 severity classes) and R², RMSE
    for continuous DI regression.

    Ground truth: 0-6 pustule rating scale scored on 20 plants per plot at 5
    sampling points, converted to Disease Index (DI%), grouped into 6 severity
    classes. Validated with ASD FieldSpec 4 handheld spectroradiometer
    (2151 bands, 350-2500 nm).

    The final output is a geo-referenced severity classification map with
    spray/monitor/skip zones.

    Key references: Tian et al. (2021) — DSSF framework; Feng et al. (2022) —
    multi-source fusion R²=0.872; Liu et al. (2024) — thermal+spectral fusion;
    Maimaitijiang et al. (2020) — multi-sensor advantage for crop assessment.


18. What data will your solution require? *

    (1) UAV imagery from four sensors, collected over the experimental field:
        - RGB (DJI Phantom 4 Pro): 3-band, 8.2 cm GSD at 30 m AGL
        - Multispectral (MicaSense RedEdge MX): 5 bands (Blue, Green, Red,
          Red-Edge, NIR), 2 cm GSD, radiometrically calibrated
        - Hyperspectral (Headwall Co-Aligned VNIR-SWIR): 491 bands, 400-2500 nm,
          9.7 cm GSD (VNIR), radiometrically and atmospherically corrected
        - Thermal (FLIR Vue Pro R): 7.5-13.5 um, 2 cm GSD, with atmospheric
          temperature correction for absolute canopy temperature

    (2) Ground truth disease severity data: visual pustule ratings (0-6 scale)
        scored on 20 plants per plot at 5 sampling points per plot, recorded at
        multiple growth stages during the crop season.

    (3) Ground-truth spectral measurements: ASD FieldSpec 4 handheld
        spectroradiometer readings (2151 bands, 350-2500 nm) for calibrating
        UAV-derived features against laboratory-grade spectra.

    (4) Field metadata: plot coordinates (GPS), inoculation treatment details,
        variety information, sowing and scoring dates, weather data (temperature,
        humidity, rainfall) from the IARI meteorological observatory.

    (5) For validation (Tier 1 and Tier 2): UAV imagery and ground-truth data
        from IIRMR Bharatpur, Rajasthan (Season 2) and from farmers' fields in
        the Bharatpur region (Season 2), to test model transferability across
        sites, genotypes, and management practices.

    All data is collected as part of the ongoing PhD research at ICAR-IARI.
    No external or third-party datasets are required for the core pipeline.

SECTION F: IMPACT & SCALABILITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

21. What impact could your solution create? *

    For farmers: RustWatch replaces subjective, untimely manual scouting with
    an objective, repeatable, UAV-based disease assessment. The severity map
    tells farmers exactly where disease pressure is high and where to spray —
    enabling targeted fungicide application that can reduce chemical use by
    15-30% while protecting yield. Early detection (before staghead) preserves
    the intervention window, preventing the 20-60% yield losses that occur
    when disease is caught late.

    For science: RustWatch produces the first systematic benchmark of
    multimodal sensor fusion for white rust in mustard. By comparing single-
    sensor baselines against multi-sensor fusion combinations, we establish
    which sensor pairs (and which disease-specific features) provide the
    greatest discriminative power. This fills a critical knowledge gap
    identified by Terentev et al. (2022) and Maimaitijiang et al. (2020).

    For policy: District-level severity maps aggregated from UAV flights
    support evidence-based crop insurance assessment, disease surveillance
    by state agriculture departments, and targeted allocation of plant
    protection resources. This aligns with India's Digital Agriculture Mission
    and the NMEO-OS goal of strengthening mustard productivity.

    For the environment: Precision spraying based on severity zones reduces
    the total volume of fungicide applied per season, lowering chemical
    residues in soil and groundwater, and reducing farmer exposure to
    agrochemicals.

    SDG alignment: SDG 2 (Zero Hunger) — protecting crop yield; SDG 12
    (Responsible Production) — precision input use; SDG 15 (Life on Land) —
    reduced chemical pollution.


22. How will your solution benefit small and marginal farmers and/or other
    underserved actors in agri-food systems? *

    Small and marginal mustard farmers (1-2 ha holdings, typical of Rajasthan
    and UP) cannot afford blanket fungicide application, and they lack the
    expertise to judge disease severity accurately. RustWatch is designed to
    reach them through the intermediaries they already interact with:

    (1) Drone service providers: Custom Hiring Centres (CHCs) and private drone
        operators can offer disease diagnosis as a paid service. The farmer pays
        for one UAV flight; RustWatch processes the imagery and delivers a spray
        map. The output is not a spectral index but a plain-language
        recommendation: "spray here, skip there."

    (2) FPOs and cooperatives: Farmer Producer Organizations can pool resources
        for a single drone survey covering all member fields, making per-farmer
        cost negligible. The severity map enables collective procurement of the
        exact quantity of fungicide needed — no more, no less.

    (3) KVKs and extension officers: Krishi Vigyan Kendras can use the severity
        maps for village-level crop health advisories, replacing the current
        approach of issuing district-wide blanket spray recommendations.

    (4) Language and literacy: The final output is a colour-coded map (green =
        safe, yellow = watch, red = spray). No technical literacy is required to
        interpret it. At the hackathon, we will add plain-language advisory text
        in Hindi alongside the map.

    By reducing unnecessary spraying and catching disease early, even a 10%
    yield saving on a 1-hectare mustard plot (average yield ~1.5 t/ha, MSP
    Rs 5,650/quintal) translates to Rs 8,000-10,000 additional income per
    farmer per season — significant for smallholders.


23. How could the solution be scaled or replicated across other regions,
    countries, commodities, or value chains? *

    Geographic scaling: The core pipeline (feature extraction, selection,
    fusion, classification) is not hard-coded to IARI's field conditions. By
    retraining the ML models on local data, the system adapts to any mustard-
    growing region. Our three-tier validation plan (IARI → IIRMR Bharatpur →
    farmers' fields) explicitly tests this transferability across agro-climatic
    zones, genotypes, and management practices.

    Commodity scaling: The multimodal fusion framework is crop- and disease-
    agnostic. The same 4-sensor acquisition, DSF extraction, and feature-level
    fusion pipeline transfers directly to:
    - Wheat rust (Puccinia spp.) — the largest cereal disease globally
    - Rice blast (Magnaporthe oryzae) — major threat in Asian rice systems
    - Alternaria blight in mustard — another major Brassica disease
    - Downy mildew in rapeseed-mustard — co-occurs with white rust
    Only the ground-truth labelling and DSF selection need retraining; the
    pipeline architecture remains identical.

    Sensor scaling: The feature-level fusion approach works with any combination
    of sensors. For large-area monitoring, multispectral satellite data
    (Sentinel-2, 10 m resolution) can replace UAV multispectral for district-
    level screening, with UAV deployed only for confirmed hotspots — a
    hierarchical surveillance model.

    Delivery scaling: The severity map output can be served through existing
    farmer advisory platforms (Kisan Suvidha, mKisan, IFFCO Kisan) or through
    drone service providers operating under India's Drone Rules 2021. One
    trained drone operator with a processing laptop can serve 50-100 farmers
    per season.


24. How could the solution be sustained beyond the Hackathon? *

    (1) Research continuity: RustWatch is the core of an ongoing PhD thesis at
        ICAR-IARI under the supervision of Dr. Rabi Narayan Sahoo (Principal
        Scientist, Division of Agricultural Physics). The research has
        institutional funding, sensor infrastructure, and a multi-season,
        multi-location validation plan extending to 2027-28. The hackathon
        accelerates, but does not create, this research programme.

    (2) Institutional embedding: ICAR-IARI is India's premier agricultural
        research institute with direct linkages to the ICAR extension network
        (700+ KVKs), state agriculture departments, and ICAR research stations
        across the mustard belt. Validated models and protocols will be
        disseminated through ICAR's existing technology transfer mechanisms.

    (3) Publication and open science: The fusion benchmark, DSF database, and
        trained models will be published in peer-reviewed journals (target:
        2027) and released as open-source code, enabling other researchers and
        developers to build upon the work.

    (4) Service model: Drone service providers and FPOs can offer per-acre
        disease diagnosis and spray prescription as a paid service, creating a
        sustainable revenue model. The processing pipeline runs on a standard
        laptop — no cloud infrastructure or subscription is required.

    (5) Policy integration: IARI's proximity to policymakers (DARE, Ministry
        of Agriculture) and Dr. Sahoo's role as a co-organiser of this
        hackathon positions RustWatch for integration into national crop health
        surveillance programmes and the Digital Agriculture Mission.
